NodaLab is a web application for learning music theory, which I have been designing and developing on my own since 2025, with a view to selling it.

You build a graph of nodes: a scale feeds its chords, a chord feeds a guitar fretboard, a rhythm feeds a track, a track feeds a player. The same graph is shown in two ways:
- an editor, a zoomable canvas of draggable nodes linked by typed connections (the colour of a port tells what it accepts);
- a page, a layout of interactive widgets meant for the learner.

Everything can be heard: chords are arpeggiated, scales run up and down, rhythms play and the fretboard lights up in time with the sound (Web Audio with Tone.js, MIDI import, instrument samples). Explanation pages, translated into the reader's language, open in the editor itself so that they can be taken apart and copied.

On the server side: Node.js, Express and SQLite, user accounts with Google and GitHub sign-in, messaging, multilingual content. The project is covered by unit and end-to-end tests.

Galeri3 is a studio of the LabEx IMU in Lyon devoted to the arêtes de poisson ("fish bones"), a network of underground tunnels of unknown origin beneath the Croix-Rousse hill, which it studies across several disciplines.

For this studio, I developed a web application, based on ud-viz, where its members collaborate on geolocated 3D data:
- conversations attached to a place, to discuss right on the data;
- user management, with an administrator role that grants access rights;
- 3D data import, stored on the server.



The code is open source: GALERI3 repository on GitHub.

For the LabEx IMU in Lyon, I developed UD-IMUV, a multiplayer web game that serves as a virtual meeting place for the laboratories and partners of the IMU community. It stays open at all times to the city's stakeholders, professionals and citizens alike, who take an interest in urban planning.

The game runs on the engine I wrote in ud-viz. Each player controls an avatar on a floating island above Lyon.


It features:
- a conference room, with a dynamic model for exploring urban data;
- collaborative studios, which players create to study the city;
- a chat to talk with the other players;
- an exhibition room devoted to urban planning.



The code is open source: UD-Imuv repository on GitHub.
The project was the subject of a scientific paper: IMUV: A Digital Twin for Mediation to Discover and Exchange on Territories.

Guess It generates series of quizzes: guess a film from a still or a redacted plot summary, a song from an audio clip, a country from its flag or from a map that slowly zooms out, a painter from a painting… About fifteen kinds of content, which can be filtered by decade, genre, country or popularity.

The quiz is rendered as a video right in the browser: each frame is drawn on a canvas then encoded to MP4 (WebCodecs), and playback starts while encoding is still under way. The video can be watched full screen or downloaded.



The data comes from a dozen public APIs (TMDb, Wikidata, Wikipedia, iTunes, PokeAPI, Open Trivia DB…) and from Wikipedia dumps. A refresh process queries them through rate-limited request queues and fills an SQLite cache; the Express server only reads this cache, without any network call.

A "quiz of the day" mixes anniversaries (releases, births) and current trends, the same for every player until midnight.

Subtitle Game is a multiplayer subtitling game. Players watch a foreign-language film clip and write their own subtitles, usually absurd ones, on their phones.

They then take turns projecting them on a shared screen, where the video is synchronised in real time for everyone (Socket.IO). The best versions are saved, edited together on a timeline, then exported as a film with burned-in subtitles.

All the video goes through ffmpeg: cutting new clips from a film library, converting SRT subtitles to WebVTT, burning them in and concatenating the clips for the final render.

The Node.js server (Express, Socket.IO, JWT sessions) can be deployed on Linux or shipped as a standalone Windows executable; the client is written in plain JavaScript, without a framework. An interactive tutorial welcomes new players.



The code is available on GitHub.
Meteoblocks is a game developed in one week during The Artists Tavern Game Jam - Summer'25, where it won the best gameplay award. There were three of us: an artist, a developer and me, on music.

It is a Tetris where each block is made of an element that reacts to the weather: you have to consider its nature before placing it, because the weather changes during the game. Completing lines unlocks bonuses.

I composed four tracks, one for each kind of weather.

Covidjam is a multiplayer game developed in 48 hours with Unity, during the second COVID lockdown in France, at a remote game jam among friends. There were three developers and two artists; I worked on every part of the code, and we designed the gameplay together.

Each player roams the streets of a city to rally protesters: the bigger their crowd grows, the more residents they win over to their cause.

Souk is a multiplayer game developed in 48 hours with Godot, at a game jam among friends. There were three developers, two artists and a musician; I worked on every part of the code, and we designed the gameplay together.

Players blend into the crowd of NPCs in a bustling souk. They must quietly pick up the diamonds scattered around the market without being spotted, and unmask then eliminate their opponents before those collect enough of them.

Steam Pong is a multiplayer game developed in 48 hours during the Global Game Jam 2014, by a team of three: an artist, a developer and me, on sound.

Two players face off in the spirit of Pong by sending units at each other: each unit that crosses the opponent's line scores points. To stop one, you have to send a unit of the right colour against it, following a rock-paper-scissors logic.

I composed two industrial electronic tracks and created the sound effects. The game received positive feedback from the Global Game Jam jury.
